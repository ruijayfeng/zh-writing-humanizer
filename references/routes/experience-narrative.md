# Experience Narrative Diagnosis

Read this only when the user asks to plan or diagnose the structure of a public
article based on product use, experiments, or projects. Ordinary article
drafting and rewriting use the public-account route without this reference.

## Read Before Mapping

Start from the supplied prose, notes, screenshots, prompts, results, and
corrections. A clean case summary can check factual coverage, but it need not
dictate the article's sequence or voice. Do not replace original materials
with a rewritten case card before drafting.

If a map would help, record only the decisions and observations that actually
matter to this article. The map is an editor's scratchpad, not a deliverable or
required outline. Omit it when the material already has a readable movement.

## Structural Questions

- What makes the writer care about this task, according to supplied material?
- Which result or action is worth showing first?
- Where does the reader need a screenshot, prompt, technical explanation, or
  detour to understand what happened?
- Which later action was actually prompted by an earlier result?
- Which cases add a different observation, and which repeat one already made?
- What remains unresolved or less certain at the end?

These are questions, not slots to fill. An article may be a long experiment
tour, a workflow story, a notebook of several projects, or something else. The
cases need not have equal length, matching headings, or a common moral.

## Avoid Overconstruction

Do not make every event a setup, surprise, interpretation, and lesson. Do not
give every defect the same role in proving one thesis. When a result already
shows its consequence, let the reader see it and move on. Preserve side paths
and uneven detail when they help explain the work.

Fix overconstruction by restoring concrete material and varying the article's
movement, not by imposing a shorter word count. A long article can remain
alive when each passage adds a useful observation, decision, image, or change
of view.

The author may be excited, irritated, amused, or surprised when the supplied
experience supports it. Do not require a verbatim quote for every natural
reaction, but do not invent scenes, outcomes, or emotions merely to make a
structured summary sound lived in.
