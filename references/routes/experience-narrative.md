# Experience Narrative Route

Use this reference for public articles built from product trials, workflows,
projects, experiments, or several related cases. It selects an article shape;
it is not a fixed outline and does not authorize invented experience.

## Build Case Cards First

Before building case cards, classify the supplied material:

- **raw trace:** notes written during the work, transcripts, prompts, screenshots,
  corrections, discarded attempts, messages, timestamps, or the author's own
  unpolished wording;
- **fact record:** verified actions, conditions, results, defects, and limits;
- **editorial card:** material already normalized into expectation, action,
  result, reaction, next step, and conclusion.

An editorial card is useful for fact coverage but weak evidence for voice. Do
not expand its `reaction` field into a vivid first-person scene unless a raw
trace supports the wording or behavior. When only editorial cards are
available, prefer plain reporting and mark the missing voice evidence instead
of manufacturing lived texture.

For each supplied case, locate only what the material supports:

- what put the task in the writer's hands;
- the concrete object, screen, file, image, or result a reader can picture;
- what the writer expected;
- the action that materially changed the result;
- the first observable result;
- a surprise, defect, useful friction, or limitation;
- what the writer did next;
- what judgment changed, strengthened, or became narrower;
- what question naturally followed.

Missing fields stay missing. Do not invent a failure, joke, conversation,
reader reaction, or emotional beat to complete the card.

Preserve uneven evidence. A real trace may contain an abandoned path, a result
the author did not understand immediately, a boring check, or a detail that
does not complete the thesis. Do not force every retained event to prove the
same judgment.

## Choose The Movement

Common shapes include:

- **Workflow reuse:** one complete routine teaches the reader the mechanism;
  later cases show where else it transfers.
- **Experiment tour:** a small test establishes a baseline; later tests raise
  difficulty, change the object, or expose a boundary.
- **Problem, intervention, expansion:** a visible bad result identifies the
  weak link; a new tool or method fixes it; later cases show the fix traveling.
- **Multi-project notebook:** different projects examine different sides of
  one question, while each project still contains a result and an interpretation.

Use another shape when the material demands it. Do not force one main story,
three examples, equal chapters, or a complete taxonomy.

## Draft Local Experiment Loops

The useful unit is usually short:

```text
question or desire → consequential action → visible result
→ reaction or interpretation → next action or bounded judgment
```

Open a section with the object or event when possible. Put technical mechanism
near the result it explains. A screenshot, prompt, or output should arrive when
the reader knows what to inspect; return to prose afterward to explain what it
changed.

Not every result needs drama. A small change of plan, discarded input, failed
recording, awkward output, unexpected convenience, or useful limitation can
make the writer present because it affects the next move.

Do not close every loop in prose. If the action and result already make the
consequence visible, move on. As a default editing check, allow at most one
explicit interpretation in a section; use more only when the mechanism would
otherwise be misunderstood. Delete a takeaway sentence when it merely names
the lesson the previous scene already showed.

Sections may be uneven. One may be a short observation, another a longer
debugging sequence, and a third an unresolved note. Do not give every section
the same question → action → result → lesson cadence or a heading that previews
its twist.

## Keep The Voice Alive Without Imitation

Public writing can be openly excited, amused, frustrated, skeptical, or
pleased. Preserve reactions that are present in the material and connect them
to their cause. Do not import another creator's greeting, emoji rhythm,
catchphrases, calls to engagement, or signature punch lines.

Prefer behavioral evidence over emotion labels. An author's repeated click,
deleted input, abrupt change of plan, quoted note, or decision to stop can carry
more voice than `我很惊喜`, `有点好笑`, or `这让我意识到`. Use those labels only
when the supplied author actually used them or the surrounding trace makes the
reaction unambiguous.

Author continuity can help when it is real: an earlier project, a recurring
work habit, a previously stated preference, or actual reader feedback may
explain why this test mattered. Leave it out when no such material exists.

## Revision Check

Before polishing sentences, ask:

1. Can a reader name what the writer actually did in every retained case?
2. Does each section add a new observation, pressure, transfer, contradiction,
   or conclusion?
3. Did at least one result influence the next action or final judgment?
4. Are reactions caused by events rather than added as decoration?
5. Would the article still move if greetings, emojis, and formatting vanished?
6. Is the movement suspiciously complete: every problem escalates the same
   thesis, every section ends with a lesson, and every heading announces a
   turn? If so, restore raw sequence, cut interpretation, or admit that the
   source is too normalized for a lively first-person narrative.
7. Underline every sentence that explains what the preceding event `说明`,
   `意味着`, or `让我意识到`. If removing it loses no factual or decision
   information, delete it.

If not, reorder or merge cases before doing surface humanization.
